Why is FaceTime so quiet on my Mac — and how to fix it

By Eduard Bruch8 min readUpdated July 2026
ENDEFRES日本語+28

You're on a FaceTime call, the other person is talking, and you've cranked your Mac to full volume — but you can still barely hear them. Then a notification chimes and nearly blows your speakers. Here's why it happens, and how to fix it for good.

The short version: macOS gives every app the same system volume. FaceTime, your music, Slack pings, a YouTube tab — they all share one slider. So when FaceTime's own audio is mastered quietly, your only lever is the master volume, which turns everything up with it.

Why FaceTime ends up so quiet

FaceTime prioritises clarity and echo-cancellation over loudness. Combined with a quiet-talking caller and the Mac's small built-in speakers, the result is a call that sits well below your music or videos. Turning up the master volume "works" — until any other sound arrives at that same, now-much-louder level.

  • The caller's microphone gain is low on their end.
  • macOS applies a single output volume to every app at once.
  • Built-in Mac speakers have limited headroom for voice.
  • Notification and system sounds share that same slider.
The problem was never your Mac. It's that macOS never gave you a volume knob per app.

The proper fix: per-app volume

Instead of riding the master slider, set FaceTime louder than everything else and leave it there. With a per-app mixer you can push FaceTime to 100%, keep music at 60%, and drop notifications to 20% — so a Slack ping at 2 a.m. never detonates mid-call.

Open the mixer from your menu bar, find FaceTime in the list, and slide it up. Save it as a "Calls" profile so one click restores those exact levels whenever you jump on a call. Press ⌥ F1 and you're set.

If you'd rather not install anything

You can partly work around it: raise the caller's mic on their side, use headphones (which have their own amplification), or mute notifications with Focus while you're on a call. None of it gives you true per-app control — but it takes the edge off until you set up a mixer.
